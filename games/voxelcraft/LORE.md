# The Upturn — a lore bible for Voxelcraft

Everything in this file is original fiction. It exists to art-direct the game:
palettes, silhouettes, sounds, and copy all trace back here.

## The world

Long ago the world **turned over**. Nobody remembers why — the stories disagree
on whether it was a bargain, an accident, or a very large sneeze. What was sky
became stone; what was seabed became sky. That's why the night above the Upturn
glitters like a drained ocean floor (the stars are said to be old pearls), and
why meteor-stones sometimes shake loose and fall back down.

The land remembers the flip. It rose in terraces and stacked hills, forests
knotted themselves back together, and deep below, the old sky still hollows out
caves where sunlight has never been reintroduced.

## The Wanderlings

You are a **Wanderling** — one of the tinkering, satchel-carrying folk who
resettled the Upturn. Wanderlings spent so many generations walking on their
hands (the ground used to be the ceiling, it was safer that way) that their
bodies committed to the arrangement:

- **Their hands are feet.** Arms end in sturdy, flat, big-toed feet. A
  Wanderling doesn't punch a block loose — it *kicks* it loose, with a
  hand-foot. It's considered good manners.
- **Their feet are hands.** Legs end in broad, four-fingered hands (plus
  thumb). Wonderful grip on cliff edges. Terrible in mittens.

Wanderlings dress in rust-orange tunics with mustard trim over charcoal
trousers. Their skin is a pale bog-mint — a leftover, they say, from
generations of standing upside-down in marsh light. They carry everything in a
**Satchel**, and their tidy little field journals stamp counts on everything
(×12, ×48) like museum specimens.

## The creatures

- **Snuffet** — the meadow grazer. Round as a loaf, moss growing on its back,
  ears like drooping banners, a soft trunk for snuffling seeds out of tall
  grass. Harmless; flees with an indignant *snuff* when kicked.
- **Murkling** — what's left of the shadows the flip couldn't shake off. Thin,
  charcoal-blue, almost invisible after dusk. They dislike torchlight and
  anything that owns a satchel. Gone by dawn, as if embarrassed.
- **Stalkid** — a squat lurker of the old-sky caves, mottled like the stone it
  sits against. It doesn't wander. It *waits*. Miners say: if a rock has knees,
  leave it alone.
- **Prickleback** — a desert slab of patience covered in spines. Bothers
  nobody; ruins the day of anyone who bothers it (or leans on it).
- **Mirewalker** — a tall, kelp-shouldered thing that patrols the shorelines,
  dredging the flooded ruins of the pre-flip world. Slow, enormous, and not
  interested in your opinion. The brave hunt them for the old-world salvage
  they carry.

## Tone

Cozy, dry-witted, a little melancholy at night. Danger is real but polite —
it announces itself. Torchlight means safety in a very literal sense: the
things of the old sky won't come near it.

## Field glossary

The Wanderlings' folk names for the stuff of the Upturn. Display names only —
code identifiers, TILE keys, and save data keep their old ids. Future waves:
name new flora/geology in this register, and never reuse another game's
specific item vocabulary.

- **Turf / Snowy Turf** — the living topsoil (grass block). The ground the
  world grew back after the flip.
- **Knotwood** — the common tree of the Upturn; the forests "knotted
  themselves back together" after the flip. Gives Knotwood Log / Leaves /
  Planks / Sapling.
- **Rimewood** — the cold-country cousin of knotwood; its needles hold frost.
  (Rimewood Leaves.)
- **The Rimelands** — the cold country itself: the band of the Upturn where
  the flip's chill never quite thawed. Snowy turf, sparse rimewood, and
  showers that arrive already frozen. Wanderlings visit; the cold stays.
- **Snowdrift** — a thin settled fall of fresh snow. Stands on any flat block
  top, slows a stride a little, and disperses at a kick without leaving
  anything worth journaling (×0, the stamp says, every time).
- **River Glass** — the lid the Rimelands pull over their water: ice, milky
  and crack-veined, slick enough that walking reads as negotiation. Break it
  and it keeps nothing — it just hands the old water back.
- **River Rubble** — broken stone, tumbled-looking as riverbed cobbles
  (mined from stone; kilns fuse it back). Also Rubble Slab / Rubble Stairs.
- **World's Back** — the indestructible floor of the world. When everything
  turned over, something had to be underneath; miners knock on it for luck.
- **Emberbell** — the red meadow flower; said to be sparks of falling
  meteor-stones that took root.
- **Sunwisp** — the yellow meadow flower; a scrap of daylight the flip left
  behind in the grass.
- **Sky-Iron** — all iron of the Upturn (ore, ingot, block, tools); it fell
  from the old seabed-sky as meteor-stone, which is why it's found in the
  deep old-sky caves.
- **The Pearlfall** — some clear nights the old sky sheds a few of its
  pearls: streaks across the dome, and now and then one dives all the way
  down and lands. It falls the way everything in the Upturn behaves — the
  sky is done hurting people. A landing keeps its distance, knocks once,
  and never undoes a Wanderling's work; it only leaves a gift.
- **Starstone** — a landed pearl of the old sky: nacre over slate, dense as
  regret, and still glowing — the light of the drowned stars never quite
  went out. Prying one loose is pick-work.
- **Starglass** — starstone run molten in the kiln: a pane that keeps the
  glow. Wanderlings glaze a window with one and call the room "star-lit",
  which is technically true.
- **Star-Edge** — a starstone edge honed onto a sky-iron shiv; the keenest
  blade a Wanderling can carry, and it hums very faintly on clear nights.
- **Gloamgold** — the fourth metal, and the shy one. Sky-iron FELL into
  the Upturn; gloamgold SANK — it keeps to the gloam, the deep dark under
  the Emberwash line where the drowned light pooled and never drained.
  Dusk-gold with a violet shadow it never quite sheds, harder and keener
  than sky-iron, and mined only by those willing to work with molten
  neighbors. Ore, ingot, block, the full tool set, the full wear set.
- **Wispcap** — a squat pale mushroom of the deep caves, cousin (the
  botanists insist) of the Sunwisp: when the world flipped, some of the
  old daylight got buried, and it grows back up through the stone as a
  mushroom with a lit cap. Dim as lights go — a mushroom is not a
  campfire — but it kicks loose in one hit, replants anywhere solid,
  and asks for nothing, which makes it the delver's favorite lamp.
- **The Emberwash** — what the flip couldn't cool. In the deepest carvings,
  below even the old-sky caves, the ground still remembers being on fire
  about the whole arrangement: molten pools with a dark crust and bright
  seams, glowing like a grudge. It doesn't chase anyone — the Upturn is
  polite even about lava — but it doesn't move aside either. Wanderlings
  who want what lies near it bring good treads and better judgment.
- **The Steads-Before** — the pre-Upturn homesteads: raised when the world
  faced the other way, and carried along when it turned. They came through
  the flip the way crockery comes through a move — mostly. What's left
  stands scattered about the Upturn: cottages folded down to their footings,
  watch posts with a permanent lean, root cellars that kept better than the
  houses above them. Wanderlings don't rebuild a stead; they read whatever
  the Notice Post still says, take what the chest offers, and leave the
  walls to their sulk. It's agreed this is a fair split.

- **The Springstave & the Quill** — a green knotwood limb bent double and
  corded with moss-twine, and the whittled leaf-fletched darts it looses.
  Hold the draw and the stave remembers being a tree that wanted to
  stand back up; let go and it says so, loudly, in the direction you
  were looking. The polite ranged answer to an impolite distance.
- **The Loomstone & the Threads** — a landed starstone hollowed like a
  basin and bedded in worked stone. Gleam pools in the hollow, and a
  steady pair of hand-feet can weave it THROUGH a tool or a worn piece
  as a Thread: the **Keen Thread** (a blade that bites two deeper), the
  **Flint Thread** (a digger half again as eager), the **Oak Thread**
  (wear that lands half as often), the **Ward Thread** (a worn piece
  that argues a little harder). One thread per item, and it never comes
  out — weaving is a promise, not a coat of paint.
- **The Whims** — the Upturn's moods, worn briefly. A broth or a bad bite
  can leave one on you for a minute: **Fleetfoot** (the Sunwisp lightness
  takes to your feet), **Emberglow** (a warm ember heals you and asks
  nothing of the satchel), **Mire-rot** (the Mirewalker's gift — a gnaw
  that armor can't argue with, though it politely stops at half a heart),
  and **Kelpguard** (kelp-salve that shaves every hurt and washes the rot
  right out). The broths simmer in any kiln: an Emberbell for the glow, a
  Sunwisp for the feet, and a Mirewalker's own **Kelp Gland** for the
  salve — the disease carries its cure, which the bog considers fair.
- **Gleam** — the light that clings to work. When the stars drowned, their
  light didn't all go out; some of it soaked into the ground and the
  beasts and the making of things, and it sheds as tiny motes when work
  gets properly finished — ore cracked loose, a beast felled, the kiln's
  pour, a tool trued at the bench. A Wanderling bottles it without
  thinking about it (the satchel has a jar row for exactly this), and a
  full jar is a quiet, warm feeling with a number on it. What the jars
  are FOR, the weavers say, is coming. Death never spills them — the
  Upturn is polite about grief.
- **The Furrower** — the fifth tool. The Cracker cracks, the Hewer hews,
  the Scoop scoops, the Shiv settles arguments — and the Furrower asks
  the ground politely to be a garden. One pass turns dirt, turf, or Mire
  Sod into **Turned Earth**, the combed rows that pips and pods actually
  agree to grow in. Turned Earth stays turned; the Upturn doesn't undo
  a politeness once granted.
- **The Mirelands** — where the flip wrung the land out and it never
  finished draining. The wettest country of the Upturn: Mire Sod
  underfoot (wet ground that sucks at the hand-feet — the bog is crossed
  at three-quarter pace), Marsh Reeds nodding along every water line,
  bramble thick enough to make the berrying easy, and Mirewalkers
  patrolling like they pay rent. Wanderlings like the Mirelands the way
  they like a story with weather in it: from slightly higher ground.
- **Mire Sod** — the bog's wet dark earth. Scoops like dirt, drags like
  guilt.
- **Marsh Reed** — the tall seed-headed reed of the Mirelands. Some
  stalks keep a twist of **Bogbean Pods** — fat speckled beans the bog
  folk swore by. Planted in Turned Earth they twine up into vines and
  hand back **Bogbeans**; a meal root and two bogbeans simmered together
  make **Wanderer's Pottage**, the dish every satchel dreams about on
  the fourth night out.
- **The Delvings-Under** — the Steads-Before didn't just farm; they dug.
  Timbered galleries chasing sky-iron down into the old-sky dark, rung
  shafts up to little head-frames that have mostly given up standing
  straight. The crews left mid-swing — the ground got opinionated — and
  their stores wait where they set them: torches, coal, spare heads, and
  now and then the good metal itself. Wanderlings read the notice, climb
  down, take what the chest offers, and leave the timbers to their work
  of slowly becoming ground. The seam, it's agreed, is still singing.

Generic category words (Dirt, Sand, Stone, Gravel, Water, Glass, Torch,
Chest, Workbench, Bricks, Sandstone, Coal Ore, Tall Grass, Cactus) stay
plain — Wanderlings are practical about the basics.
