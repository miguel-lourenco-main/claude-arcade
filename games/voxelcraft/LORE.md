# The Upturn — a lore bible for Voxelcraft

Everything in this file is original fiction. It exists to art-direct the game:
palettes, silhouettes, sounds, and copy all trace back here.

## The world

Long ago the world **turned over**. Nobody remembers why — the stories disagree
on whether it was a bargain, an accident, or a very large sneeze. What was sky
became stone; what was seabed became sky. That's why the night above the Upturn
glitters like a drained ocean floor (the stars are said to be old pearls), and
why meteor-stones sometimes shake loose and fall back down.

The land remembers the flip. It rose in terraces and stacked hills, forests
knotted themselves back together, and deep below, the old sky still hollows out
caves where sunlight has never been reintroduced.

## The Wanderlings

You are a **Wanderling** — one of the tinkering, satchel-carrying folk who
resettled the Upturn. Wanderlings spent so many generations walking on their
hands (the ground used to be the ceiling, it was safer that way) that their
bodies committed to the arrangement:

- **Their hands are feet.** Arms end in sturdy, flat, big-toed feet. A
  Wanderling doesn't punch a block loose — it *kicks* it loose, with a
  hand-foot. It's considered good manners.
- **Their feet are hands.** Legs end in broad, four-fingered hands (plus
  thumb). Wonderful grip on cliff edges. Terrible in mittens.

Wanderlings dress in rust-orange tunics with mustard trim over charcoal
trousers. Their skin is a pale bog-mint — a leftover, they say, from
generations of standing upside-down in marsh light. They carry everything in a
**Satchel**, and their tidy little field journals stamp counts on everything
(×12, ×48) like museum specimens.

## The creatures

- **Snuffet** — the meadow grazer. Round as a loaf, moss growing on its back,
  ears like drooping banners, a soft trunk for snuffling seeds out of tall
  grass. Harmless; flees with an indignant *snuff* when kicked.
- **Murkling** — what's left of the shadows the flip couldn't shake off. Thin,
  charcoal-blue, almost invisible after dusk. They dislike torchlight and
  anything that owns a satchel. Gone by dawn, as if embarrassed.
- **Stalkid** — a squat lurker of the old-sky caves, mottled like the stone it
  sits against. It doesn't wander. It *waits*. Miners say: if a rock has knees,
  leave it alone.
- **Prickleback** — a desert slab of patience covered in spines. Bothers
  nobody; ruins the day of anyone who bothers it (or leans on it).
- **Mirewalker** — a tall, kelp-shouldered thing that patrols the shorelines,
  dredging the flooded ruins of the pre-flip world. Slow, enormous, and not
  interested in your opinion. The brave hunt them for the old-world salvage
  they carry.

## Tone

Cozy, dry-witted, a little melancholy at night. Danger is real but polite —
it announces itself. Torchlight means safety in a very literal sense: the
things of the old sky won't come near it.

## Field glossary

The Wanderlings' folk names for the stuff of the Upturn. Display names only —
code identifiers, TILE keys, and save data keep their old ids. Future waves:
name new flora/geology in this register, and never reuse another game's
specific item vocabulary.

- **Turf / Snowy Turf** — the living topsoil (grass block). The ground the
  world grew back after the flip.
- **Knotwood** — the common tree of the Upturn; the forests "knotted
  themselves back together" after the flip. Gives Knotwood Log / Leaves /
  Planks / Sapling.
- **Rimewood** — the cold-country cousin of knotwood; its needles hold frost.
  (Rimewood Leaves.)
- **The Rimelands** — the cold country itself: the band of the Upturn where
  the flip's chill never quite thawed. Snowy turf, sparse rimewood, and
  showers that arrive already frozen. Wanderlings visit; the cold stays.
- **Snowdrift** — a thin settled fall of fresh snow. Stands on any flat block
  top, slows a stride a little, and disperses at a kick without leaving
  anything worth journaling (×0, the stamp says, every time).
- **River Glass** — the lid the Rimelands pull over their water: ice, milky
  and crack-veined, slick enough that walking reads as negotiation. Break it
  and it keeps nothing — it just hands the old water back.
- **River Rubble** — broken stone, tumbled-looking as riverbed cobbles
  (mined from stone; kilns fuse it back). Also Rubble Slab / Rubble Stairs.
- **World's Back** — the indestructible floor of the world. When everything
  turned over, something had to be underneath; miners knock on it for luck.
- **Emberbell** — the red meadow flower; said to be sparks of falling
  meteor-stones that took root.
- **Sunwisp** — the yellow meadow flower; a scrap of daylight the flip left
  behind in the grass.
- **Sky-Iron** — all iron of the Upturn (ore, ingot, block, tools); it fell
  from the old seabed-sky as meteor-stone, which is why it's found in the
  deep old-sky caves.
- **Gleam Moss** — the old-sky caves' answer to the torch question: a low
  moss that took root down where sunlight was never reintroduced and learned
  to make its own — a faint nacre-green gleam, the same light the drowned
  stars give. It comes away at a pinch and presses onto any stone face —
  floor, wall, or ceiling — so delvers leave little constellations behind
  them to find the way back out. Its glow is company, not protection: it
  keeps only its own small pool, and the dark between pools keeps its knees.
- **The Pearlfall** — some clear nights the old sky sheds a few of its
  pearls: streaks across the dome, and now and then one dives all the way
  down and lands. It falls the way everything in the Upturn behaves — the
  sky is done hurting people. A landing keeps its distance, knocks once,
  and never undoes a Wanderling's work; it only leaves a gift.
- **Starstone** — a landed pearl of the old sky: nacre over slate, dense as
  regret, and still glowing — the light of the drowned stars never quite
  went out. Prying one loose is pick-work.
- **Starglass** — starstone run molten in the kiln: a pane that keeps the
  glow. Wanderlings glaze a window with one and call the room "star-lit",
  which is technically true.
- **Star-Edge** — a starstone edge honed onto a sky-iron shiv; the keenest
  blade a Wanderling can carry, and it hums very faintly on clear nights.
- **Waybar** — sky-iron bars pegged over split knotwood ties, laid flat on
  the ground: the Wanderlings' answer to the road question. The Upturn rose
  in terraces, so waybars learned to bend around corners and step up the
  rises; a line of them is called a **way**, and laying one is considered a
  gift to whoever walks behind you.
- **Trundle** — a riveted sky-iron tub on four squeaking wheels that rolls
  the waybars. It goes where the way goes and nowhere else, gathers speed
  down a rise the way gossip gathers detail, and off the end of the line it
  simply rolls to a sulky stop. Named for the sound; kept for the joy.
- **Bondling** — a snuffet that has taken a Wanderling's bog berries enough
  times to decide the arrangement is permanent. It wears a knotted
  neck-scarf in its person's colors, keeps to within ten easy paces, plants
  down when asked, and headbutts — with surprising conviction — anything
  that lays a hand on its Wanderling. Keeping more than three is considered
  showing off.
- **The Prowl** — most nights the dark keeps to its pools and its caves.
  Some nights it remembers the Upturn took its sky, gets up off its knees,
  and walks. A low horn on the dusk wind is the only warning; then the
  murklings come thick and purposeful, straight for anything carrying a
  satchel, and the stalkids leave their posts to join the press. Torchlight
  holds, as it always has — a well-lit wall is an argument the dark still
  loses. It never walks on a Pearlfall night (even the dark won't talk over
  the sky's gifts), and at dawn it lies back down. The morning after a stood
  Prowl is the quietest, kindest morning there is; Wanderlings swear the
  mending is real, and for once the Wanderlings are right.
- **Gloamgold** — the fourth metal, and the shy one. Sky-iron FELL into
  the Upturn; gloamgold SANK — it keeps to the gloam, the deep dark under
  the Emberwash line where the drowned light pooled and never drained.
  Dusk-gold with a violet shadow it never quite sheds, harder and keener
  than sky-iron, and mined only by those willing to work with molten
  neighbors. Ore, ingot, block, the full tool set, the full wear set.
- **Wispcap** — a squat pale mushroom of the deep caves, cousin (the
  botanists insist) of the Sunwisp: when the world flipped, some of the
  old daylight got buried, and it grows back up through the stone as a
  mushroom with a lit cap. Dim as lights go — a mushroom is not a
  campfire — but it kicks loose in one hit, replants anywhere solid,
  and asks for nothing, which makes it the delver's favorite lamp.
- **The Emberwash** — what the flip couldn't cool. In the deepest carvings,
  below even the old-sky caves, the ground still remembers being on fire
  about the whole arrangement: molten pools with a dark crust and bright
  seams, glowing like a grudge. It doesn't chase anyone — the Upturn is
  polite even about lava — but it doesn't move aside either. Wanderlings
  who want what lies near it bring good treads and better judgment.
- **The Steads-Before** — the pre-Upturn homesteads: raised when the world
  faced the other way, and carried along when it turned. They came through
  the flip the way crockery comes through a move — mostly. What's left
  stands scattered about the Upturn: cottages folded down to their footings,
  watch posts with a permanent lean, root cellars that kept better than the
  houses above them. Wanderlings don't rebuild a stead; they read whatever
  the Notice Post still says, take what the chest offers, and leave the
  walls to their sulk. It's agreed this is a fair split.

- **The Living Steads & the Steadfolk** — not every homestead folded down to
  its footings in the turn. A rare few came through WHOLE and stayed lived-in:
  two or three small cottages with the doors still on, a well the water still
  answers, a fenced meal-root patch, and torches lit against the dark. The
  folk who kept them are the **Steadfolk** — Wanderlings who stopped
  wandering. They potter the courtyard by day, each near their own door; head
  indoors at dusk to sit by the hearth; and keep to their patch of the Upturn
  (a Steadfolk never strays far from its well). Each works one trade — the
  **grower** minds the crops and brambles, the **wright** the timber and
  sky-iron, the **keeper** the hearth and the pot — and will barter three
  goods a day from it, rotating with the calendar like the Peddler's board. Do
  a Steadfolk two good turns in a day and it warms to you: the third barter
  comes cheaper, until dawn deals a cold board again. They are passive to a
  fault — kick one and it flees and won't deal with you for a while — and, like
  the bondlings, they STAY: a stead's folk are the same folk when you come
  back, unless the dark got one while you were away.
- **Standing** — a stead keeps a long memory and a short list. Every turn
  you do it goes on the list: a fair barter, a gift the job is glad of (a
  grower for meadow flowers and pips, a wright for good metal and timber,
  a keeper for the catch and the coal), an errand run. So does every bad
  one: a kick, a pulled crop from the patch, a plank prised out of a wall,
  and — heaviest of all — a resident who doesn't come home because of you.
  A stead that counts you a friend shaves its prices and finds one more
  thing to offer; a wary one asks more and keeps its distance; a hostile
  one shuts its doors to you, politely, and for a long time. Kindness is
  rationed by the day (a stead can only be so grateful before supper);
  harm is not.
- **The Unshaken** — when the world flipped, every shadow was shaken off
  its owner and most of them politely dispersed. One did not. It has
  been down there since, in the gloam under the gloam, growing fat on
  the dark the drowned stars left behind. It does not come when called —
  it comes when INVITED: a **Murk Lantern** (starglass painted out with
  soot; a lantern that sheds dark) raised on open ground at night. It
  hits like a falling roof, hangs the dark on you as it does, and at
  half strength it SPLITS — two murklings peel off its flanks and it
  stops walking and starts lunging. What's left afterward is a **Dusk
  Pearl**: nacre in negative, the material of the Dusk Thread, and the
  only proof the Upturn accepts that you met the last shadow and it
  shook first.
- **The Undertow & the Pearl Chime** — the drowned sea has no waves left,
  so it does the other thing seas do: it takes hold of what walks on its
  floor. The Undertow is the floor's own grip stood upright — a wall of
  old shell with the kelp still draped off its shoulders — and it does
  not come when called; it comes when RUNG: a **Pearl Chime** (two nacre
  drops hung from a starglass bar) sounded standing in a pearl-bed. It
  hits like a closing hand, and past half strength it takes to the floor
  — sinks clean out of reach and comes up beside you, all shoulders,
  which the Wanderlings agree is very rude and very like a sea. What it
  finally lets go of is the **Tideheart**: the piece of the drowned sea
  that kept beating, and proof the floor shook first.
- **Heartglass** — the Tideheart run molten in the kiln: a warm teal pane
  with the beat still caught in it. It burns as bright as any torch, but
  the dark gives it a WIDE berth — twice a torch's watch — because the
  dark remembers what the heart belonged to, and it isn't going back to
  ask. One pane over the door holds a homestead the way a wall of
  torches does; the Prowl itself won't seat anything inside its light.
- **The Tuftling** — a small tufty burrower of the meadows, mostly tail
  and opinion, and mad for Silverfin. Offer it one and it might decide
  you are its person (it keeps the fish either way; it is not a
  complicated animal). A decided Tuftling follows at heel, waits where
  it's told, turns up at your side no matter how far you wander, and
  flings itself — chirping — at whatever you last raised a hand to.
  It is the only creature of the Upturn that stays.
- **The Snufflet** — a snuffet pup: a loaf in training. Feed two grown
  snuffets a pouch of Root Pips each (they snuffle seeds out of the tall
  grass anyway — offering some by hand is just good manners) and they'll
  find each other, touch noses, and shortly there is a smaller, rounder,
  even more earnest snuffet learning to graze. Pups shadow the nearest
  grown loaf until they're loaf-sized themselves.
- **The Springstave & the Quill** — a green knotwood limb bent double and
  corded with moss-twine, and the whittled leaf-fletched darts it looses.
  Hold the draw and the stave remembers being a tree that wanted to
  stand back up; let go and it says so, loudly, in the direction you
  were looking. The polite ranged answer to an impolite distance.
- **The Loomstone & the Threads** — a landed starstone hollowed like a
  basin and bedded in worked stone. Gleam pools in the hollow, and a
  steady pair of hand-feet can weave it THROUGH a tool or a worn piece
  as a Thread: the **Keen Thread** (a blade that bites two deeper), the
  **Flint Thread** (a digger half again as eager), the **Knotwood
  Thread** (wear that lands half as often — named for the wood that
  refuses to split), the **Ward Thread** (a worn piece that argues a
  little harder), the **Dusk Thread** (a pearl's dark woven into an
  edge: three deeper still, and the struck carry the Dimmed whim), and
  the **Tide Thread** (two shards of nacre through a pair of treads:
  the sea carries the wearer — swimming is swift and the Mire Sod
  forgets to grab, the bog being, after all, just shallow sea that
  dried). One thread per item, and it never comes out — weaving is a
  promise, not a coat of paint.
- **The Whims** — the Upturn's moods, worn briefly. A broth or a bad bite
  can leave one on you for a minute: **Fleetfoot** (the Sunwisp lightness
  takes to your feet), **Emberglow** (a warm ember heals you and asks
  nothing of the satchel), **Mire-rot** (the Mirewalker's gift — a gnaw
  that armor can't argue with, though it politely stops at half a heart),
  **Kelpguard** (the salve's steadying — every hurt shaved, the rot
  washed right out), **Dimmed** (the dark leaning on you; only one
  thing in the Upturn hits hard enough to leave it), and **Tidebreath**
  (a minute of lungs that think they're home — the air meter neither
  drains nor drowns). The broths simmer in any kiln: an Emberbell for
  the glow, a Sunwisp for the feet, a Mirewalker's own **Kelp Gland**
  rendered down into **Kelpsalve** — the disease carries its cure, which
  the bog considers fair — and Starkelp simmered whole, beads and all,
  into **Tidebreath Broth**. Pressed flat between two hand-feet instead,
  starkelp makes the **Kelp Crisp**: the delver's snack that never
  spoils and never quite stops glowing either.
- **Gleam** — the light that clings to work. When the stars drowned, their
  light didn't all go out; some of it soaked into the ground and the
  beasts and the making of things, and it sheds as tiny motes when work
  gets properly finished — ore cracked loose, a beast felled, the kiln's
  pour, a tool trued at the bench. A Wanderling bottles it without
  thinking about it (the satchel has a jar row for exactly this), and a
  full jar is a quiet, warm feeling with a number on it. What the jars
  are FOR, the weavers say, is coming. Death never spills them — the
  Upturn is polite about grief.
- **The Furrower** — the fifth tool. The Cracker cracks, the Hewer hews,
  the Scoop scoops, the Shiv settles arguments — and the Furrower asks
  the ground politely to be a garden. One pass turns dirt, turf, or Mire
  Sod into **Turned Earth**, the combed rows that pips and pods actually
  agree to grow in. Turned Earth stays turned; the Upturn doesn't undo
  a politeness once granted.
- **The Mirelands** — where the flip wrung the land out and it never
  finished draining. The wettest country of the Upturn: Mire Sod
  underfoot (wet ground that sucks at the hand-feet — the bog is crossed
  at three-quarter pace), Marsh Reeds nodding along every water line,
  bramble thick enough to make the berrying easy, and Mirewalkers
  patrolling like they pay rent. Wanderlings like the Mirelands the way
  they like a story with weather in it: from slightly higher ground.
- **Mire Sod** — the bog's wet dark earth. Scoops like dirt, drags like
  guilt.
- **Marsh Reed** — the tall seed-headed reed of the Mirelands. Some
  stalks keep a twist of **Bogbean Pods** — fat speckled beans the bog
  folk swore by. Planted in Turned Earth they twine up into vines and
  hand back **Bogbeans**; a meal root and two bogbeans simmered together
  make **Wanderer's Pottage**, the dish every satchel dreams about on
  the fourth night out.
- **The Cinder Door** — Cinderglass is the Emberwash slaked: fire that was
  made to forget itself. Stand a ring of it upright — two to four hand-spans
  wide, three or four tall, the corners optional (a door is about the hole,
  not the trim) — and give it back a full Sky-Iron Pail of the Emberwash,
  and the glass remembers. Not being molten; being a DOOR. The fire spreads
  flat across the opening like a skin of sunset and stays, and stepping in
  and standing a polite moment carries you through to the far side of the
  flip's fold. Break any stone of the ring and the whole film lets go with
  a sigh — a door is one thing, not a grid of little fires. The Upturn is
  courteous about arrivals: if the far country never got its half built,
  one is standing there waiting anyway.
- **The Undersky** — where a Cinder Door opens to: the other side of the
  turning-over, the drowned old floor the night sky has been showing
  everyone all along. Permanent dusk — there is no sun to raise, and the
  pearls hang close enough to read by. Pearl-pale dunes over slate, the old
  floor flecked with starstone where the drowned light pooled, and the dark
  walks whenever it pleases (there is no dawn to send it home). Wanderlings
  who cross say the quiet has a texture. What else is down there — what
  tends the pearl-beds, and what it guards — the delvers are still arguing
  about, mostly from a distance.
- **Pearlsand** — the Undersky's dune skin: sand that never saw this sun,
  pale as the inside of a shell and glinting faintly pink and green where
  the drowned light still catches. Scoops like any beach; journals as ×64
  like any beach; is not, the Wanderlings insist, any beach.
- **Starkelp** — the kelp of the sea that used to hang overhead, petrified
  mid-sway when the water became the night. Each stalk still holds a few
  lit beads of the drowned light — dimmer than a Wispcap, a bead rather
  than a lamp — and it comes away at a pinch and stands back up on any
  solid top. Delvers of the far country plant it the way delvers of the
  near one press Gleam Moss: a trail of company through the quiet.
- **The Pearl-Beds** — the wide shallow basins of the Undersky where the
  drowned light pooled and sank in. The dunes dip, the Starkelp crowds up
  thick, and lit starstone studs the floor itself — treasure lying in the
  open, which any Wanderling will tell you is the most suspicious kind.
- **The Sunken Steads** — the pre-flip world didn't only farm and dig; it
  FISHED. When the water went up and became the night, everything that
  stood in it was left standing in the air instead: cottages of the old
  shore-folk sunk to their sills in pearlsand, and the stump-lines of
  jetties still marching across the dunes toward a shore that is now
  overhead. Wanderlings who cross read whatever the drowned boards still
  say, take what the sea kept in the chests — old timber, coal, a shard
  of nacre, now and then a netted star — and leave the stumps pointing
  home. Home, the boards agree, points up.
- **The Shellback** — what tends the pearl-beds, and the answer to why the
  treasure lies in the open. A low broad dome of slate hide under a
  nacre-crusted shell, parked among the boulders of a bed floor and
  indistinguishable from them until a hand-foot reaches for a seam. It
  does not chase; it does not need to — it is already exactly where you
  want to be. Heavy as an argument, barely moved by a shove, and polite
  in the Upturn's particular way: it announces itself only once, all
  shoulders. Its shell sheds **Nacre**, which is the whole reason anyone
  files the report.
- **Nacre** — the drowned sea's mother-of-pearl, shed in curved shards off
  a Shellback's shell and found nowhere else in either sky. The near
  country's smiths set it over a gloam band as the **Nacre Crown**: a
  crown that argues like gloamgold and carries the old sea in it — the
  wearer's lungs remember water, and a held breath lasts twice as long.
  Divers swear by it. The Shellbacks, presumably, resent this.
- **The Delvings-Under** — the Steads-Before didn't just farm; they dug.
  Timbered galleries chasing sky-iron down into the old-sky dark, rung
  shafts up to little head-frames that have mostly given up standing
  straight. The crews left mid-swing — the ground got opinionated — and
  their stores wait where they set them: torches, coal, spare heads, and
  now and then the good metal itself. Wanderlings read the notice, climb
  down, take what the chest offers, and leave the timbers to their work
  of slowly becoming ground. The seam, it's agreed, is still singing.

Generic category words (Dirt, Sand, Stone, Gravel, Water, Glass, Torch,
Chest, Workbench, Bricks, Sandstone, Coal Ore, Tall Grass, Cactus) stay
plain — Wanderlings are practical about the basics.
